This assessment uses the completed 65-checkpoint T-maze diagnostic and the subsequent frozen intervention and CAGE2 pilot. Source records live under the main checkout's `runs/minihack_tmaze_representation_diagnostics_20261126/` and `runs/frozen_memory_integration_20261126/`. ## T-maze categorical bottleneck and downstream sensitivity

All three final checkpoints encoded the reset cue in observations, embeddings and posterior logits and probabilities at 100% held-out linear accuracy. Opposite-cue argmax states coincided exactly. Sampled-state decoding was approximately 50%, consistent with equal-prior single-draw Bayes upper bounds of 50.07–50.18% with known level and conditionally independent categorical factors. Lag-8 recurrent and combined-state decoding was at chance under the original filtering conditions. This establishes the earliest bottleneck at the hard categorical state. The original figure's “observation” trace describes the encoder embedding.

The intervention picked two hard reset states using only fit-level posterior draws. They differed in five or six factors and were then injected throughout the fixed held-out histories. Swapping the injected states reversed the signed effects. Matched recurrence at lag 8 remained linearly decodable at 100% under separately fitted factual and swapped probes, and its paired L2 was 0.55–1.18% of the lag-1 separation across seeds. Every factual, swapped and cue-neutral greedy branch accuracy was 50%. At the junction the identical-history and irrelevant-marker reruns matched the intact outputs exactly for h, right probability, critic value and branch reward margin. Cue-neutral pairs had zero within-pair contrast for these readouts. These checks cover the specified marker and intervention. Across the same geometry, held-out pairs gave identical per-seed contrasts at the stored precision, so the pair-bootstrap intervals were degenerate.

**Reward prediction.** A correct immediate branch model would reverse the sign of the predicted right-minus-left reward margin when the true cue reverses. The simulator-margin contrast is +4 across factual cue pairs. The one-step prior-mode margins stayed positive for both cues, with no task-aligned contrast at a meaningful scale. This provides additional evidence of a downstream reward-readout deficiency under the intervention. It leaves open whether the prior transition further attenuated the distinction, whether the reward head failed to use it, or how sampled prior successors would behave.

**Actor and critic.** The probability measurements show negligible local branch-policy response for these states. They add evidence beyond the unchanged greedy action. The scalar online critic also changed little, but equal cue-conditioned values can be appropriate when both cues offer equally good choices. The intervention therefore cannot independently diagnose critic learning. The selected states may be rare under the ordinary posterior, and the same two states were injected across levels. The result establishes local behavior for these interventions and checkpoints. It cannot establish global insensitivity or causal defects in the training objectives.

**Candidate mechanisms.** Reset reconstruction averaged 99.1% over cells and was 50% at the cue cell. The eight-row supervised replay window never contained both reset and terminal reward. The terminal-reward window saw reset only through detached burn-in, which eliminates the direct within-window gradient to reset encoding. Replay truncation, reconstruction weighting and objective design remain candidate causes.

## Bounded CAGE2 pilot

**GO for archive compatibility and bounded flat-checkpoint validation. NO-GO for broad checkpoint evaluation or hierarchy comparisons.** The 360-episode archive reproduced the historical held-out observation-only return probe at R² 0.3136861160 and MAE 7.9618190742. The matched flat checkpoint reproduced all 720 archived greedy decisions, step rewards, available reward components and truncation flags over 24 B-line episodes. Privileged active-session labels were collected separately and joined offline. This validates the recorded subset and leaves the historical behavioral estimates intact.

- **Encoding and compression.** On the fit-selected host's active-session target, held-out balanced accuracy was 98.08% from current observations and encoder output, 100% from raw logits, pre/post-unimix probabilities and argmax, and 95.98% from sampled z. The visible-compromise auxiliary target gave 100% from embeddings, 96.30% from posterior probabilities, 90.00% from argmax and 92.92% from sampled z with the linear probes. Effects depend on the diagnostic target. The T-maze categorical collapse did not recur for this CAGE2 target.
- **Retention.** Adding h or hz to current observation plus time improved balanced accuracy by 1.92 percentage points, with a paired interval of 0–4.35 points. The same gain held against the observation-plus-fixed-history baseline. Five test episodes provided 26 positive frames across four episodes. The observation baseline was near ceiling and time alone reached 90.32% balanced accuracy, so the result leaves the incremental memory effect unresolved.
- **Readout sensitivity.** Replacing recurrent history while holding the current observation and incoming action fixed changed greedy actions on 17.33% of held-out frames, with mean actor total variation 0.1767 and mean absolute online value change 9.50. This establishes sensitivity to the carry for the tested trajectories. The intervention does not identify which specific historical information drove the readouts or whether the changes would affect defensive outcomes. The recorded categorical JS and Bayes bound distinguish factual versus fixed carry. They do not measure optimal decoding of compromise.
- **Reconstruction and credit assignment.** Aggregate observation-bit accuracy was 98.03%, compared with 93.13% exact visible-compromise field accuracy and 53.59% on positive compromise fields. These are BlueTable fields. The saved replay held 6,451 complete 31-row episodes. None of the 16-row windows supervising the final stored row included reset or reached it through eight rows of burn-in. An earlier late nonzero reward could share a supervised window with reset in 6,451 of 103,216 eligible starts. This structural audit does not estimate gradient magnitude or establish a cause for representation quality.

The next minimum scientific comparison is a predeclared flat-checkpoint holdout with more independent episodes, enough positive and negative episode support, and a target stratified where current observations and time are weak. Test history increment under the same observation and incoming-action controls before interpreting a more expressive sequence probe.

## Implications for temporal attention

In T-maze the main loss occurs before the recurrent history is built. For the forced matched prefix, later observations and actions supply no additional cue evidence. Attention over that hard-state history cannot undo the single-draw information bottleneck. The already-decodable soft posterior is the smallest comparator for testing a bypass. The reset injection shows enough downstream attenuation and weak readout response to make cue preservation and its training signal the higher-priority mechanisms to investigate. These results do not show that a bypass alone would improve control.

In CAGE2 the pilot already recovered the selected target from argmax, and current observations were near ceiling. The pilot does not support either bypassing categorical compression or adding attention. Improve the temporal test first, using independent support and an offline target where current observation and time are weaker. A sequence probe would establish recoverability only. Keep readout interventions and behavioral evidence separate.
